\subsection{\ac{MPC} Challenges in Marine Robotics Autonomous Inspection Tasks}
%\label{subsec:mpc_barriers}

While MPC improves autonomous capabilities, there are fundamental challenges that could hinder its performance and deployment in marine environments. One major issue is the simulation-reality discrepancy: existing underwater simulators often lack both visual realism, which is critical for perception algorithms, and accurate physics in modeling hydrodynamic forces. This gap makes pre-deployment controller validation unreliable and forces costly trial-and-error field testing. Additionally, environmental uncertainty further complicates predictive control. Disturbances such as waves and winds exhibit complex, multi-scale spatiotemporal variations that are difficult to model accurately, resulting in discrepancies that degrade the predictive performance of traditional MPC and can lead to rapid deterioration of overall system performance. Handling tether constraints introduces another layer of complexity. Many marine vehicles are tethered, requiring planners and controllers to explicitly account for the tether to avoid entanglement and mission failure. Simultaneously optimizing inspection coverage while preventing tether entanglement remains a major challenge, as the tether is inherently difficult to model accurately and to incorporate effectively within MPC frameworks. Finally, most existing MPC approaches treat inspection as a trajectory tracking problem, relying on precomputed paths that assume static or predictable conditions. This decoupling between perception and control limits robustness because the planned trajectory may become suboptimal or infeasible in dynamic or uncertain environments.
