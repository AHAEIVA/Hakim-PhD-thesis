\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

% Applications & motivation
Marine robotics is becoming crucial for a wide variety of applications in the maritime industry, from deep-sea surveying to aerial monitoring of offshore installations. As the offshore industry scales up, it places greater demands on the use of robotic systems subsea to ensure efficient, safe, and continuous operations. Autonomy in these operations is becoming crucial not only to reduce operational expenses but also to guarantee consistent high-quality performance with minimal human oversight.

% Problem statement
Advanced real-time control and planning algorithms, such as model predictive control (MPC), are key in enabling the autonomy of marine robots, as it can handle nonlinear systems with high degrees of freedom.  Despite its potential, the integration of MPC into marine robotic systems presents new challenges related to modeling accuracy, computational efficiency, and robustness to uncertain environments.


% scope of thesis 
This dissertation addresses key challenges in achieving autonomy for marine and aerial robotic systems, with a particular focus on robots used in inspection tasks. It examines problems related to path planning and optimal control, and investigates how path planners and data-driven methods can be integrated with optimization techniques to enhance operation in complex and uncertain environments.


% contributions summary
The key contributions of this work focus on enabling the effective use of MPC in marine robotics. First, an open-source marine robotics simulator is developed, providing highly realistic rendering and physics that enable safe, cost-effective, and efficient testing of algorithms, as well as synthetic data generation. Second, novel MPC-based coverage path planning methods are proposed that take into account tether constraints while ensuring complete area coverage during inspection tasks. Third, to improve the accuracy and robustness of underwater vehicle control, advanced data-driven modeling techniques are employed, including Gaussian processes and physics-informed neural networks, to capture complex and rapidly changing underwater dynamics, thereby enhancing the overall performance and reliability of MPC algorithms.

% how contributions will help in the big picture
By integrating data-driven models and novel inspection path planners with MPC, this work enables the development of more reliable, safer, and highly adaptive algorithms for autonomous marine inspection. In addition, the introduction of a high-fidelity simulator supports a more reliable development process through realistic testing. These findings advance the state-of-the-art in marine robotics and contribute towards the real-world deployment of fully autonomous inspection and surveying operations in complex marine environments.

\chapter*{Resum\'e}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Resum\'e (Danish)}
Marinerobotik bliver stadig mere afgørende for en bred vifte af anvendelser i den maritime industri, fra dybhavskortlægning til luftovervågning af offshore-installationer. Efterhånden som offshore-industrien vokser, stilles der større krav til brugen af robotter under vandet for at sikre effektive, sikre og kontinuerlige operationer. Autonomi i disse operationer bliver essentiel, ikke blot for at reducere driftsomkostningerne, men også for at sikre konsekvent høj kvalitet med minimal menneskelig overvågning.

Avancerede realtidskontrol- og planlægningsalgoritmer, såsom model-predictive control (MPC), er nøglen til at muliggøre autonomi for marinerobotter, da de kan håndtere ikke-lineære systemer med høje frihedsgrader. På trods af deres potentiale indebærer integrationen af MPC i marinerobotiske systemer nye udfordringer relateret til modelpræcision, beregningseffektivitet og robusthed over for usikre omgivelser.

Denne afhandling adresserer centrale udfordringer i at opnå autonomi for marine og luftbårne robotsystemer, med særlig fokus på robotter brugt til inspektionsopgaver. Den undersøger problemstillinger relateret til sti-planlægning og optimal kontrol, og undersøger hvordan stiplanlæggere og datadrevne metoder kan integreres med optimeringsteknikker for at forbedre operationen i komplekse og usikre miljøer.

De væsentligste bidrag i dette arbejde fokuserer på at muliggøre effektiv brug af MPC i marinerobotik. For det første udvikles en open-source marine robotiksimulator, som tilbyder meget realistisk gengivelse og fysik, hvilket muliggør sikker, omkostningseffektiv og effektiv test af algoritmer samt generering af syntetiske data. For det andet foreslås nye MPC-baserede dækningstiplanlægningsmetoder, der tager højde for tether-begrænsninger og samtidig sikrer fuldstændig dækning af områder under inspektionsopgaver. For det tredje anvendes avancerede datadrevne modelleringsmetoder, herunder Gaussian processer og fysikinformerede neurale netværk, for at forbedre præcisionen og robustheden i kontrol af undervandsfartøjer ved at fange komplekse og hurtigt skiftende undervandsdynamikker, hvilket øger MPC-algoritmernes samlede ydeevne og pålidelighed.

Ved at integrere datadrevne modeller og nye inspektionsstiplanlæggere med MPC muliggør dette arbejde udviklingen af mere pålidelige, sikrere og stærkt adaptive algoritmer til autonome marine inspektionsopgaver. Derudover understøtter introduktionen af en højpræcis simulator en mere pålidelig udviklingsproces gennem realistisk testning. Disse resultater fremmer state-of-the-art inden for marine robotteknologi og bidrager til den virkelige implementering af fuldt autonome inspektions- og kortlægningsoperationer i komplekse marine miljøer.
